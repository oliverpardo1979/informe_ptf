\begin{abstract}
Colombia's near-zero aggregate total factor productivity (TFP) growth conceals economic activities moving in different directions. We reconstruct the long-run evolution of TFP for the total economy and nine activities using the annual production-account results published by the national statistical office (DANE). We chain the 20 annual log growth rates from 2005 to 2024, recover the annual aggregation weights implicit in the publication, validate them against nominal gross-output shares, and decompose aggregate TFP into exact activity contributions. The aggregate TFP index fell from 100 in 2004 to 98.5 in 2024, equivalent to an annualized rate of $-0.073$ percent. Trade TFP increased by 23.2 percent, while mining TFP fell by 32.1 percent. Four activities contributed 0.315 percentage points per year to aggregate TFP, but five activities subtracted 0.389 points. Thus, aggregate stagnation was the net result of offsetting positive and negative sectoral contributions. An accounting counterfactual that sets annual TFP growth to zero in the five activities with negative long-run performance raises production-account output growth from 3.26 to 3.65 percent per year. The exercise measures an accounting margin, not a causal policy effect or a counterfactual path for GDP.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and Replication Files}

The DANE source annexes, processed data, replication programs, figures, and table sources are available in the project repository:

\url{https://github.com/oliverpardo1979/informe_ptf}.

\noindent The spreadsheet calculations behind the six figures and the paper's principal results are contained in the six sheets whose names begin with \texttt{CJC} in the
\href{https://github.com/oliverpardo1979/informe_ptf/blob/main/outputs/ptf_pesos_vbp_20260727_financieras/anex-PTF-Productividad-2025_con_calculos_CJC.xlsx}{primary TFP calculation workbook}.
Those sheets report the aggregate value-added decomposition, chained activity indices, annual gross-output weights, sectoral contributions, counterfactuals, and the source range for each figure. The sheet \texttt{CJC Gu\'ia} provides a map of the calculations and distinguishes source data from formulas.

\noindent The same six calculation sheets are also included in the
\href{https://github.com/oliverpardo1979/informe_ptf/blob/main/outputs/ptf_pesos_vbp_20260727_financieras/anex-PTF-ProductividadLaboral-2025_con_calculos_CJC.xlsx}{companion labor-productivity workbook}.
This second file is a convenience copy, not an independent source of calculations or results: the two workbooks retain different original DANE tables but share the same \texttt{CJC} calculation package. The original DANE annexes are redistributed in the repository for reproducibility; users should consult DANE for the official releases and any subsequent revisions.
